How the maintenance cycle works

  1. External monitoring: decide which outside warnings apply.
  2. Internal monitoring: know your assets, settings, and actual access.
  3. Planning and risk assessment: connect evidence to business consequences.
  4. Assessment and remediation: validate, correct, and verify weaknesses.
  5. Readiness and review: rehearse, learn, and improve the program.

Read the full BrewByte scenario and decision rules

Scenario and decision rules

Business and roles

BrewByte operates two fictional coffee shops. Checkout depends on register devices. Online ordering depends on ORDER-01 and DB-01. GW-01 supports vendor administration. BKP-01 stores backups. Guest Wi-Fi should provide Internet access without access to checkout administration.

RoleResponsibility
Business OwnerOwns business priorities, funding, and acceptance of material residual risk
IT LeadOwns network, gateway, backups, and approved technical changes
Application OwnerOwns ordering and database services
Store Systems OwnerOwns register devices and secure retirement evidence
Vendor CoordinatorOwns vendor and partner access reviews
Store Operations LeadOwns staff onboarding, offboarding, and store continuity procedures

These are role labels, not real people. The Business Owner has the scenario's authority to accept material remaining risk. Technicians provide evidence and options.

Classroom operating requirements

These are invented policy requirements for this exercise, not external mandates.

  • Vendor administration requires a named account, MFA, explicit approval, and an enforced end time.
  • Guest Wi-Fi must not reach checkout management interfaces.
  • Departed staff access must be disabled when employment ends, and completion must be verified.
  • The delivery partner should see only assigned delivery details needed for the service. Bulk access to customer profiles is outside the approved purpose.
  • Audit logging must follow the approved baseline.
  • Retirement requires evidence of custody, data handling, and credential/access removal.
  • The online ordering service has an RTO of 60 minutes and an RPO of 60 minutes.
  • High-priority confirmed findings require a response plan within one business day. A due date is a target for this fictional exercise, not proof the target is appropriate for every organization.
  • Production changes require recorded approval, pre-change testing, a rollback plan, and post-change verification. Emergency work uses an authorized emergency route with documentation and follow-up.

How to reason about the data

Inventory records declare what the organization knows. Missing evidence creates uncertainty. A retired status alone does not prove data erasure. A successful backup does not prove successful restoration. A vendor advisory indicates possible exposure, not that an incident occurred.

Use the asset ID to join records. Compare configuration using the pair (asset_id, setting). Current-only configuration entries for new devices are new observations, not changes to an existing baseline entry.

The change log is complete for recorded approvals during the comparison period. A difference with no matching approval requires investigation. CHG-105 is conditional: enabling support does not authorize disabling MFA or omitting an expiry.

Treat the domains as cooperating functions. External information and internal facts inform risk assessment. Assessment identifies weaknesses and remediation addresses them. Rehearsal and review test whether people and processes are ready.

Which warning matters?

Turn a raw warning into a specific business decision without claiming an unproven incident.

The idea

A warning needs local context.

Match the product and affected version, then assess exposure and business consequence. A relevant warning does not prove exploitation.

Your task

  1. Compare each advisory's product and version with the current inventory.
  2. Identify the asset and finding, if any, that match the warning.
  3. Separate three statements: a version match, known exposure, and confirmed exploitation. What does the evidence actually support?
  4. Write a short internal warning including asset, business impact, immediate action, owner, and next update time.
  5. Decide how to handle the unrelated advisory without losing relevant background information.

Assess partner access

Use least privilege and verified business need to control an information exchange throughout its life.

The idea

Access should follow verified need.

Begin with the delivery service’s purpose. Limit identity permissions and data access to what that purpose requires, and plan how access ends.

Your task

  1. Review the QuickCup delivery pilot and account AC-05.
  2. State the intended business purpose and the information the partner actually needs.
  3. Write a risk scenario identifying the asset, threat, weakness, and business consequence.
  4. Propose restricted permissions, authentication appropriate to the integration, review dates, and a termination procedure.
  5. Decide what must happen before access is considered acceptable. Identify who can accept material remaining risk.
  6. Explain why having a contract alone does not establish a safe technical connection.

Prioritize and verify fixes

Prioritization needs context, and closure needs evidence.

The idea

A finding is the start of a decision.

Validate evidence, prioritize business exposure, assign an owner, and verify the outcome. A temporary restriction and a repaired defect are different results.

Your task

  1. Review all seven findings. Separate confirmed open findings, an unconfirmed report, and an evidence-supported closure.
  2. Select your first three actions and explain the ordering using exposure, business dependence, existing controls, and uncertainty.
  3. Complete a remediation tracker with an owner, temporary control if needed, target date, permanent action, and verification evidence.
  4. Specify how F-005 should be validated. Do not prescribe an unsupported product fix from a version-only suspicion.
  5. Explain why F-007 should not re-enter the patch queue without new evidence.
  6. Explain the difference between blocking exposure, repairing a defect, and formally accepting residual risk.

Field definitions

Inventory: asset_id is the stable join key; hostname is the human-readable name; business_role explains its service; location is a fictional site; product and version support advisory matching; owner_role identifies accountability; criticality is business importance; network_zone groups intended network use; private_ip is a private illustrative address; lifecycle_state records Active or Retired. Unassigned ownership is a gap for review.

Configuration: setting names the control or property; value is the approved or observed state. These tables contain observations, not commands.

Approvals: change_id is a stable reference; approved_date records the decision; change_type selects inventory or configuration; setting_or_field and approved_value identify the approved state; implementation_state describes recorded execution; approver_role and record_note give accountability and conditions. Completed describes the recorded implementation, not a guarantee that every condition was met.

Business changes: event_id identifies a business event; date is its recording date; change_description explains the event; related_assets lists relevant asset IDs; responsible_role owns the business process.

Access review: account_type identifies staff, vendor, service, or partner use; purpose describes the intended role; employment_or_contract_state gives business context; enabled describes account state; privilege_scope describes permissions; mfa records the observed authentication control; access_expiry records the enforced end time or its absence; last_review_date and review_owner_role identify review context. A service account with not-applicable MFA is not automatically defective; evaluate workload identity, privilege, and credential management separately.

Findings: finding_id is the unique record; discovered_date is the discovery date; finding_description describes the observation; validation_state separates evidence from suspicion; exposure describes reachability/context; business_impact gives a consequence; existing_control is a relevant safeguard; fix_available describes an option; due_date is a fictional target; status tracks current disposition. A severity score is deliberately omitted so you must justify priority.

Recovery: activity distinguishes a backup job, restore test, or estimate; result labels the evidence; duration_minutes means observed restore duration or an explicitly labeled estimate; for backup jobs it means job runtime. data_age_minutes means the recovery point's age at restoration or a hypothetical estimate. Zero on a backup job only describes its snapshot and does not prove recovery within RPO at an arbitrary outage time. evidence_note limits interpretation.

Incident timeline: time_et orders observations; source is a role or source system; related_assets gives context; observation describes only what the exercise establishes. A hypothesis is not a confirmed cause.

Important joins

Use asset_id across inventory, configurations, approvals, findings, and recovery records. related_assets may contain several IDs. The access-review file does not use a direct asset key: use the stated purpose and scenario to connect accounts to services.
