01 / MISSION BRIEF

You are BrewByte's cybersecurity program analyst

BrewByte is growing from three coffee shops to twelve. It uses cloud point-of-sale (POS), payroll, supplier payments, employee scheduling, loyalty accounts and managed Wi-Fi. Management has approved a cybersecurity improvement initiative—but wants proof that controls are effective.

Your assignment

Make six defensible management decisions, practice eight vocabulary applications, design a measurable KPI, and defend an executive recommendation with optional AI coaching.

Business priorities

Keep POS operating, protect payroll and customer data, prevent unauthorized payments, and control access.

Security constraints

Small IT team, limited budget, seasonal hires, outsourced support, and multiple locations.

Learn before you decide

Management practice: a repeatable process that turns security policy into operational behavior. Control: a safeguard designed to reduce risk. Metric: evidence that helps evaluate whether a control is implemented or effective. Benchmark: a point of comparison, not an implementation blueprint.

The next sections cover employment lifecycle controls, separation of duties, measurement design, quantitative analysis, benchmarking and executive reporting.

02 / PEOPLE & ACCESS

Hiring, onboarding and offboarding

Concept: Cybersecurity begins before an employee gets an account and continues through role changes and departure. HR, managers, IT and security must coordinate. Job advertisements should explain responsibilities without exposing sensitive access architecture.

Scenario: A departing store manager

At 4 p.m., BrewByte learns that a manager who can access payroll, the POS admin console, the building and a vendor portal is leaving immediately. They also have a company laptop and a personal phone with an authenticated business app.

Choose the best coordinated response:

Select an answer, then submit.

Key terms

Identification: claim an identity. Authentication: verify it. Authorization: determine allowed actions. Accountability: record actions for review. Least privilege: provide only the access needed for the role.

Discussion: How would this response change for an employee who gives two weeks' notice? What access must still expire at the actual departure time?

03 / PERSONNEL CONTROLS

Prevent a single-person failure

Concept: Separation of duties divides sensitive steps among people; two-person control requires independent review or approval. Job rotation and mandatory time away can expose hidden errors and reduce reliance on one individual.

Scenario: Vendor payment workflow

One BrewByte employee can create a new supplier, change its bank details, approve invoices and release payments. A fraudulent bank change could be difficult to detect.

Which redesign best addresses the risk?

Select an answer, then submit.

Apply the idea

Identify a second workflow where a single employee could make and conceal an unauthorized change: POS refunds, payroll, privileged access or inventory. Explain who requests, approves and audits the action.

04 / PERFORMANCE MANAGEMENT

Measure outcomes, not just activity

Concept: A good security measure is quantifiable, repeatable, feasible to collect and useful for a decision. Implementation asks whether a control exists; effectiveness asks whether it works; efficiency asks what resources it takes; impact asks what the business experienced.

06 / BENCHMARKING & DUE CARE

Choose what to improve first

Concept: External benchmarking compares BrewByte with similar organizations. Internal benchmarking compares BrewByte with its own prior results. Due care means taking reasonable protective steps; due diligence means continually verifying and maintaining those steps. A peer's control may be worth considering without copying its implementation.

Scenario: Three risks, one small team
