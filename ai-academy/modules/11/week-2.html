Module 11 · Week 2: Connect risk to evidence

BrewByte assignment: Produce a short application/system card, an AI inventory entry, data-flow map, RACI, ten-risk register, control/evidence matrix, supplier questionnaire, monitoring/incident plan, and a two-page release memo. Apply the pack to the actual student application, including known weaknesses.

Learn the concept

NIST Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage organize risk work rather than replacing judgment. Distinguish likelihood, impact, controls, and residual risk. State assumptions and evidence gaps. Fairness and privacy decisions require more than a generic checkbox or a model-generated assessment.

Worked business example

A cross-role document leak has a control owner, retrieval-denial tests, residual limitations, and a review trigger when roles or indexing change.

Guided practice

Read this week's assigned resource sections about NIST functions, impact assessment, threat/business risk, fairness limitations. Spend about three hours on reading, small examples, and explaining the vocabulary in your own words. You are not expected to complete every linked course this week.

Open the guided notebook or starter script. The notebook names the dependency and execution requirements. Run the relevant worked example and inspect its inputs and outputs. For Module 1, begin with a manual prompt comparison; coding is introduced in Module 2.

Build ten application-specific risk entries with owners, mitigations, evidence pointers, residual ratings, and review dates/triggers. Identify one risk that cannot yet be accepted.

Change one input or condition, predict the result, then run or observe it. Preserve a failing case before repairing it. Complete the task in your own learning repository rather than modifying the public teaching source.

Security boundary

Integrate privacy, fairness, transparency, data residency, retention, model/supplier change control, shadow AI, and human oversight. Use deterministic policy enforcement as the required low-compute path; optionally compare NeMo Guardrails or a suitable Llama Guard model. Evaluate their model/backend requirements and license. Guardrails are fallible and do not confer legal compliance.

Submit evidence

Ten-risk register with evidence gaps. Include commands or procedure, relevant outputs, one failed case, your interpretation, and AI-assistance disclosure. Use synthetic data only. Clearly distinguish personally executed work, observed instructor demonstrations, and pending exercises.

Check your understanding

  • Which assumption in connect risk to evidence could make your BrewByte result misleading?
  • What fails if the input is malformed, unauthorized, or missing?
  • What evidence would convince a reviewer that your change works on an unseen case?

These public questions are formative. Explain your reasoning; they are not hidden certification questions.
