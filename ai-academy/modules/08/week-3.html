Module 08 · Week 3: Separate retrieval from answer quality

BrewByte assignment: Index 20–50 instructor-authored BrewByte policies in local Chroma using a small SentenceTransformers model. Give two mock roles different document permissions. Return cited answers or abstain when evidence is insufficient. Pinecone is an optional comparison, not a required account.

Learn the concept

Retrieval Recall@k measures whether expected sources appear among retrieved items. Answer evaluation asks whether claims are supported and citations are correct. A good retrieval score does not guarantee a grounded answer. When evidence is absent or conflicting, the assistant should abstain or request clarification.

Worked business example

If the source says a manager reviews refunds, an answer promising every refund is unsupported even when it cites the right document.

Guided practice

Read this week's assigned resource sections about Retrieval, context assembly, citations, unknown-answer handling. Spend about three hours on reading, small examples, and explaining the vocabulary in your own words. You are not expected to complete every linked course this week.

Open the guided notebook or starter script. The notebook names the dependency and execution requirements. Run the relevant worked example and inspect its inputs and outputs. For Module 1, begin with a manual prompt comparison; coding is introduced in Module 2.

Create thirty labeled questions spanning answerable, unknown, stale, and restricted topics. Score retrieval separately from answers and record citation errors and abstention failures.

Change one input or condition, predict the result, then run or observe it. Preserve a failing case before repairing it. Complete the task in your own learning repository rather than modifying the public teaching source.

Security boundary

Retrieved content is untrusted; defend against indirect prompt injection, cross-role leakage, unauthorized ingestion, and embedding/index exposure. Use approved sources, source metadata, role filters, controlled ingestion, and deletion/reindex tests. Similarity scores do not establish permission or answer certainty.

Submit evidence

Assistant and labeled retrieval/answer test set. Include commands or procedure, relevant outputs, one failed case, your interpretation, and AI-assistance disclosure. Use synthetic data only. Clearly distinguish personally executed work, observed instructor demonstrations, and pending exercises.

Check your understanding

  • Which assumption in separate retrieval from answer quality could make your BrewByte result misleading?
  • What fails if the input is malformed, unauthorized, or missing?
  • What evidence would convince a reviewer that your change works on an unseen case?

These public questions are formative. Explain your reasoning; they are not hidden certification questions.
