Concept 1

Operating systems and applications

An operating system manages hardware resources and provides services to applications. Applications help users perform tasks such as sales, scheduling, reporting, and messaging.

BrewByte in practice

A register runs an operating system and a separate point-of-sale application.

Concept 2

Productivity and collaboration

Spreadsheets analyze inventory; word processors document procedures; presentations communicate plans; collaboration tools coordinate work. Shared documents require deliberate permissions and version control.

Concept 3

Licensing and deployment

Software may be proprietary, open source, licensed per device or user, or delivered as a subscription. Evaluate support, compatibility, accessibility, cost, security updates, and data handling before selection.

BrewByte in practice

Compare a hosted scheduling subscription against locally installed software using a total-cost and risk checklist.

Concept 4

Safe installation and updates

Obtain software from trusted sources, verify publishers where practical, apply approved updates, and avoid unnecessary permissions. Back up important data before disruptive changes.

BrewByte in practice

Concept 5

Remote support and access

Remote assistance should be authorized, time-limited, auditable, and scoped to the minimum required privileges. Never ask users to share passwords or bypass multifactor authentication.

BrewByte in practice

An IT technician helps a manager recover an application with an approved remote-support tool.

Concept 6

Troubleshooting methodology

Identify the problem, form a theory, test it, plan the fix, implement, verify full functionality, and document findings. Follow local change and escalation procedures.

BrewByte in practice

A register cannot print receipts after an update; check scope, queue, connectivity, and service status before replacing hardware.
